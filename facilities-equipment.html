Facilities & Equipment

Two facilities today, a third on the way — built to prototype, test, and scale. The U-M Battery Lab spans two complementary sites in Ann Arbor today, with a third comprehensive facility under construction at UMTRI. Together they offer full-service cell and module fabrication, testing, and analysis for a wide range of formats and use cases — from coin cells for early-stage chemistry work to vehicle-scale modules for industrial validation.

Battery Lab 1.0

Phoenix Memorial

Michigan Memorial Phoenix Project Building, North Campus · opened 2014 · 9,000 sq ft

Our original facility houses two independent -40°C dew-point dry rooms and two distinct lines: the Phoenix Pilot Line for industrial-scale prototyping, and the R&D Line, offering smaller-scale flexibility for early-stage chemistry and materials work. Formats supported: coin, cylindrical, and pouch cells.

  • Phoenix Pilot Line — industrial-scale prototyping
  • R&D Line — smaller-scale flexibility for early-stage chemistry and materials work
  • 500+ channels of cycle testing
  • Abuse testing
  • Characterization instruments for materials and cell diagnostics
Battery Lab 2.0

Ellsworth Road

South Ann Arbor · opened 2025 · 4,000 sq ft of lab and production space

Ellsworth was designed as a stopgap during the design and construction of Battery Lab 3.0 — and as a beachhead near the auto industry, easier for industrial users to access. It brings state-of-the-art capacity for the formats industry actually manufactures.

  • Pouch cells — up to ~60 Ah (100 × 150 mm)
  • Cylindrical cells — 21700 format, up to 5 Ah
  • Automated module & pack laser welding — Manz welder, installed and operational in 2026
  • 4,000 sq ft, -40°C dew-point dry room
  • New roll-to-roll coater and Z-fold stacker — installed in 2026
Battery Lab 3.0

UMTRI

U-M Transportation Research Institute campus · target opening 2028 · 23,500 GSF

Battery Lab 3.0 is the destination. A $60 million, 23,500-gross-square-foot addition at UMTRI will consolidate and expand the capabilities of 1.0 and 2.0 into one comprehensive, centralized facility. Regents' approval came in October 2025, the bid came in within budget, and ground broke in 2026.

  • Three battery labs under one roof
  • Dedicated welding space
  • Cycle testing at expanded scale
  • Abuse testing
  • Co-location with UMTRI, Mcity, and the broader Mobility at Michigan ecosystem
